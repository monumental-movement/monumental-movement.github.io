## Erasing a name does not mean erasing existence

Text: mmr | Theme: Why was the expression method of anonymity born, and why did it develop independently in different cities? Deciphering the influence of anonymity on music, art, and community through the activities of three groups representing Quebec and San Francisco

In the history of music, there are many artists who have hidden their true identities.

However, the purpose is not just one.

There are examples of people using masks to create commercial buzz, and there are also examples of people choosing to remain anonymous as a way to express their desire for their work to be evaluated purely. Furthermore, there are cases in which individual names have been abandoned in order to transform the community itself into a work of art.

Anonymity is not just a stage performance, but can also become a methodology for establishing the creation itself.

Since the 1980s, this idea has undergone its own evolution, especially in the North American underground.

At its heart are two regions: San Francisco on the west coast and Quebec on eastern Canada.

At first glance, the two have little in common.

San Francisco was a city of hippie culture, DIY spirit, experimental art, and performance art, while Quebec, with its own French-speaking cultural background, was a land where improvisational music, experimental music, and art intersected.

However, surprisingly similar ideas are emerging in these two places.

It is ""a feeling of discomfort in a situation where the author stands out more than the work.''

This sense of discomfort fostered a culture of anonymity in different ways.

In San Francisco, anonymity has become a celebration.

In Quebec, anonymity has quietly disappeared.

The three groups of artists compared in this article symbolize these differences.

* Angine de Poitrine
*Caroliner
*Rubber O Cement

Although their active years and production methods are different, the one thing they have in common is an attitude of not bringing the individual to the forefront.

However, the content of that anonymity is surprisingly different.

Some create fictitious ethnic histories, some transform the author into a story, and some disappear, leaving behind only their works.

In other words, there is a wide variety of ideas that cannot be explained using the single word "anonymous."

In this column, we will not simply introduce bands, but will interpret anonymity itself as an artistic concept.

The following three points are the axis of comparison.

* What did anonymity exist to protect?
* What kind of freedom did anonymity give to the work?
* How has urban culture influenced anonymous expression?

By comparing the three groups from this perspective, we can see that not having a name was not just a cover-up, but an extremely creative choice.

> In this article, we view anonymity not as a ""technique to hide one's identity," but as ""the conditions for the creation of a work itself," and compare the differences between the two.
Not only the works but also the personality itself came to be treated as a commercial value.

## 匿名文化はどこから生まれたのか

Anonymous expression did not suddenly emerge in the 1980s.

If we look back at art history, a culture that prioritizes the community over the artist's name has existed since ancient times.

中世ヨーロッパでは、多くの宗教画に作者名は残されていない。

What was important was faith, not the individual, and the work was a service to the community.

Later, in the modern era, the concept of an artist became established, and the artist's name began to have value.

From the 19th century to the 20th century, we moved into an era in which market value was determined by who made the product.

However, in the second half of the 20th century, a backlash against this idea emerged.

前衛芸術では作品を商品化する制度そのものが批判対象となった。

In performance art, the body becomes the work, and in conceptual art, the idea itself becomes the work.

Here, actions and concepts began to be considered more important than the author's name.

Furthermore, from the late 1960s to the 1970s, DIY culture spread in North America.

The idea is to record the music ourselves, print it ourselves, and distribute it ourselves.

In this culture, bypassing major labels and established media became valuable.

Anonymousness also begins to take on new meaning in this flow.

The goal is not to "become famous" but to "create things freely."

すると個人名は、むしろ創作の妨げになる場合すらあった。

It's not just music.

Collaborative production became commonplace in many fields, including zine, comics, video, installation, and improvisational theater.

It is no longer rare to find works in which we do not know who made them and to what extent.

This environment makes for particularly rich soil in San Francisco.

一方、ケベックでは少し事情が異なる。

Here, improvisational music and contemporary art are strongly linked, and a tendency has developed to place more emphasis on the ""collective sound" than on the ""individual performer."

Anonymity is not self-denial.

むしろ作品へ集中するために、自我を一歩引くという考え方だった。

Even within the same anonymity, the backgrounds differed greatly depending on the city.

## "Performance of anonymity" developed in San Francisco

アメリカ西海岸の都市サンフランシスコは、1960年代以降、実験芸術の重要な拠点となった。

Beat Generation, hippie movement, psychedelic culture, improvisation, mail art, performance art, DIY publishing.

In this city, where such different cultures developed simultaneously, the question ""What is a work?'' was always being asked.

In this context, the traditional values ​​that put the individual artist at the forefront are often called into question.

An increasing number of artists began to consider not only their works of art but also the systems surrounding them as objects of expression.

Anonymity was a very natural option in this culture.

It's not about hiding your identity.

The aim was to blur the boundaries between the work and the real world and draw the audience into the world of the work.

A live performance is not just a concert, but a ritual.

The album becomes not a sound source, but a fictitious historical document.

Posters become fragments of stories rather than advertisements.

Anonymity was a device for establishing the entire world of the work.

In San Francisco, many ideas like this were seen in the 1980s and 1990s.

A typical example is Carolinar.

On the other hand, in the same region, there were also entities that approached anonymity from a completely different direction, such as Rubber O Cement.

Even though they were in the same city, the meaning of anonymity was never the same.

> In San Francisco, anonymity grew up as an expression not for ""hiding," but for ""creating the world itself."

## Caroliner─The moment when anonymity turns into myth

Caroliner is one of San Francisco's leading avant-garde groups that has been active since the late 1980s.

The first thing that surprises people who see the work for the first time is the overwhelming amount of information it contains.

Album title.

ジャケット。

ライブ衣装。

配布物。

ステージ装飾。

All of them are consistently constructed with a worldview that resembles fictional history, folklore, or ethnography.

The story is fragmentary yet vast, and it is impossible to understand the whole picture from just one piece.

But that's exactly what Carolinar is aiming for.

They did not aim for "works to be explained."

The premise was that the audience would collect the fragments themselves and assemble the world in their own way.

At live performances, the members appear more like residents of an unknown community than humans, wearing giant masks, cloth, and handmade costumes.

誰が演奏しているのかは重要ではない。

重要なのは、その場に存在する「Caroliner」という共同幻想である。

Anonymity here is not a technology that erases the individual.

It is a theatrical device to make a fictional race appear in reality.

Music is also inseparable from that worldview.

A mix of noise, folk, improvisation, punk, and folk music elements, defying formal genre classification.

The listener receives not only the song, but the entire work as a cultural sphere.

This attitude was also typical of San Francisco, which treats everything from album production to live performances, art, and costumes as a comprehensive art form.

## Rubber O Cement──作者よりも作品が歩き続ける

Rubber O Cement embodies anonymity in a completely different way than Carolinar.

作品には一定の美学がある一方で、制作者個人について語られる情報は非常に限られている。

As a result, the listener naturally turns their attention to the work itself.

This has a slightly different meaning from the ""profile private'' often seen among modern anonymous artists.

In the case of Rubber O Cement, the goal is not to hide personal information, but rather to maintain the independent existence of the work.

つまり、「誰が作ったか」という問い自体を作品の外へ追いやっているのである。

The work has a strong sense of collage.

Recorded material.

ノイズ。

断片的な音。

environmental sounds.

Reconstruction by editing.

それらが一つの作品へ統合されながらも、制作者の人格はほとんど前景化しない。

これはDIY文化との親和性が高い。

When producing independently, we are responsible for distribution and advertising ourselves.

しかし、その目的はスターになることではない。

Continuing expression becomes a goal in itself.

Rubber O Cement is an example of putting this idea into practice in a very quiet way.

Anonymous is not a showy performance.

Rather, it was the creation of a natural environment in which works could continue to be circulated for a long time.

For this reason, even though there are people who know about the work, very few people talk about the author personally.

This structure itself embodies the idea of ​​anonymity.

> Rather than mystifying the creator, Rubber O Cement chose a state where only the work continues to exist autonomously.

---

## Even in the same city, anonymity can vary so much

CarolinerとRubber O Cementは、どちらもサンフランシスコ周辺のDIY文化を背景に持つ。

しかし、匿名という考え方には大きな違いがある。

In Caroliner, anonymity is an active production to make the story come true.

The performer becomes a character and becomes part of the world of the work.

By participating in the live performance, the audience becomes residents of that fictional world.

一方のRubber O Cementでは、匿名は演出を最小限に抑える方向へ働く。

作者は表へ出ず、作品だけが静かに残る。

There is an emphasis on autonomy rather than theatricality.

In other words, the two approaches the same question in diametrically opposed ways.

「作品は誰のものなのか。」

Caroliner replies, "It's a communal fantasy thing."

Rubber O Cementは「作品そのもののもの」と答える。

Although they both distance themselves from a view of art that centers on personal names, their methods of practice are surprisingly contrasting.

## "Quiet anonymity" fostered in Quebec

サンフランシスコでは、匿名は作品世界を拡張するための積極的な装置として発展した。

一方、ケベックではまったく異なる方向から匿名という発想が育まれていく。

Behind this is the region's unique cultural environment.

ケベック州、とりわけモントリオールは、北米の中でも独自性の強い芸術都市として知られてきた。

Although it has a French-speaking cultural background, it also has contact with American experimental music and European contemporary music, and is a place where diverse values ​​intersect.

1970年代以降、この地域では即興演奏、現代音楽、ノイズ、サウンドアート、映像、美術が密接に結び付いた。

ジャンルを明確に区切る考え方は比較的弱く、一人の表現者が複数の分野を横断することも珍しくなかった。

As a result, the boundaries between roles such as ""performer" and ""artist" and ""composer" and ""recording engineer" are becoming blurred.

Here, a work is often seen as a thought process rather than a finished product.

そのため、作者個人を強く前面に押し出す必要性は必ずしも高くなかった。

Rather, the emphasis is on leaving space in the work itself to begin a dialogue with the viewer.

The anonymity fostered in this cultural soil does not hide oneself behind flashy masks or stories.

静かに姿を消し、作品だけを残すという考え方に近い。

匿名は演出ではなく、創作態度そのものなのである。

この特徴を象徴する存在がAngine de Poitrineである。

> Québec's anonymous culture was developed not as a theatrical mask, but as an idea that quietly blended the author into the work.

## Angine de Poitrine──Creative method of "disappearing"

Angine de Poitrine is a unique figure in the Quebec experimental music scene.

His works have a consistent aesthetic.

しかし、その一方で制作者自身について語られる情報は極めて少ない。

プロフィールを積極的に発信するわけでもなく、活動そのものを神秘化する演出が前面へ出るわけでもない。

結果として、聴き手が最初に向き合うのは常に音である。

これは偶然ではない。

As you listen to his work, you can feel his precise sense of editing and compositional ability.

微細な音響。

Fragmented melody.

environmental sounds.

ノイズ。

電子音。

silence.

こうした素材が慎重に配置され、一つの音響空間を形成している。

Rather than recording an improvisational performance, it is a production approach that feels like sculpting time itself.

The sounds appear and disappear one after another, never clearly explaining anything.

That's why listeners begin to search for meaning on their own.

At this time, if the author's personality and background come to the foreground, the viewing experience may change significantly.

By removing as much unnecessary information as possible, Angine de Poitrine creates a situation where the work and the listener interact directly.

The important point here is that ""anonymity'' is not the goal.

目的は、作品そのものが独立して存在できる環境を整えることにある。

As a result, the author naturally recedes into the background.

In other words, anonymity is not a self-denial, but a choice to foreground the work.

> At Angine de Poitrine, anonymity itself is not the subject matter, but functions as a method to create an environment in which only the work can quietly stand up.

## "Disappearing author" and "appearing sound"

In art history, the concept of ""the death of the author'' is often talked about.

"Death" here does not refer to an actual person.

作品の意味を作者だけが決定するのではなく、鑑賞者との関係の中で新たに生まれていくという考え方である。

Angine de Poitrine's work has a high affinity with this way of thinking.

His works rarely present a clear story.

聴き手は音響の流れの中から、自分自身で意味を構築していく。

Therefore, each person has a different interpretation.

To some, it may sound like an echo of a city.

To others, it may give the impression of an abstract painting.

さらに別の人には、記憶の断片を呼び起こす音風景として響く可能性もある。

None of the interpretations can be unilaterally rejected.

Precisely because the artist is not in the foreground, the work is constantly updated by the experience of the viewer.

This structure is in contrast to the Carolinar.

Caroliner presents a wealth of worldviews.

The audience participates in the myth.

On the other hand, in Angine de Poitrine, the world of the work is only minimally defined.

The work is completed by the audience filling in the blanks themselves.

Although they share the commonality of anonymity, their philosophies are completely different.

## ケベックとサンフランシスコは何が決定的に違うのか

Comparing the three groups we have looked at so far, we can see that the meaning of the word anonymous varies greatly depending on the region.

サンフランシスコでは、匿名はしばしば共同体を演出するための装置となる。

Live performances, art, costumes, stories, publications, etc. come together, and the audience participates in the world.

Anonymous was a creative way to launch a "new reality."

In Quebec, on the other hand, anonymity is more like creating an environment for maintaining the transparency of works.

With the author actively disappearing, only the work exists quietly.

Here, the emphasis is on dialogue with the sound itself, rather than theatricality.

This difference is not superior or inferior.

Both have in common that they distance themselves from cults of personality.

しかし、その実践方法は文化的背景によって大きく異なる。

サンフランシスコは匿名を「付け加える」。

Quebec is "removed" by anonymous.

This contrast is the central axis of comparison in this paper.

## DIY culture and underground distribution that supported anonymity

From the 1980s to the early 2000s, in the world of experimental music, noise, and improvisational music, distribution networks that were not mediated by major record companies were formed around the world.

At the center of this was the DIY culture.

DIY here does not simply mean ""make it yourself.''

The idea is to complete recording, duplication, jacket production, distribution, sales, and promotion either by yourself or within a small community.

この仕組みは、パンク以降のインディペンデント文化とも深く結び付いていた。

Small quantity production rather than mass production.

全国流通よりも手渡し。

Word of mouth rather than advertising.

Continuation over profit.

こうした価値観は、匿名という表現方法と非常に相性が良かった。

There is no need to compete for popularity.

There is no need to post a photo of your face.

In distribution without a star system, ""what was made" is naturally more important than ""who made it."

Caroliner, Rubber O Cement, and Angine de Poitrine have all published work within this cultural sphere.

もちろん活動形態や制作方法には違いがある。

しかし、大量消費を目的としない創作姿勢という点では、共通した価値観を共有していた。

Anonymity does not mean isolation.

Rather, it was a culture that could easily be established within a small but closely connected community.

> DIY culture not only made anonymity possible, but also fostered an environment in which works could be evaluated anonymously.

## A "shelter for works" called a small label

For major labels, artist profiles and image strategies are important elements of sales promotion.

However, things are different for small labels that deal in experimental music.

作品そのものが流通の中心であり、制作者個人の情報は最小限にとどめられることも少なくなかった。

The jacket design also clearly expresses this attitude.

Abstract paintings, collages, typography, hand-drawn illustrations, or very simple designs are often used instead of flashy advertising slogans or artist photos.

こうした表現は、購入者に「作品そのものと向き合ってほしい」という意思表示でもあった。

There was also active exchange between small labels.

音源の交換、共同企画、コンピレーションへの参加などを通じて、国境を越えたネットワークが形成されていく。

In this process, trust was more important than name recognition.

As long as the quality of the work and the attitude toward activities were shared, that alone was enough.

Being anonymous didn"t necessarily mean they weren"t to be trusted.

Rather, his attitude of continually presenting his works was the foundation that supported his existence.

This is in contrast to the large-scale music industry.

> Rather than being a place to sell works, small-scale labels functioned as ""shelters'' where diverse expressions could exist safely.

## Why cassette culture encouraged anonymity

From the 1980s to the 1990s, cassette tapes became an important medium for experimental music.

カセットは安価で複製しやすく、小ロット制作にも適していた。

It was common for artists to produce works ranging from dozens to hundreds and distribute them at live concerts and via mail order.

With this distribution method, there is no need for advertising based on the assumption of mass sales.

Works pass from hand to hand to people who are interested in them.

そこでは、作品に添えられた短い解説やジャケットデザイン、あるいは口コミが重要な役割を果たした。

This system was extremely convenient for artists who worked anonymously.

顔写真がなくても問題にならない。

Even if there is little exposure to the media, the work will be distributed.

Even if he did not perform live often, he could reach fans around the world through his recorded works.

Additionally, cassette culture had an atmosphere that welcomed experimentation.

Variations in recording quality and even rough editing were often perceived as characteristics of the work.

This tolerance was also strongly linked to anonymous expression.

完璧な商品ではなく、一回限りの表現。

This sense of value is similar to the works of Migumi.

## International experimental music network connected by mail

Before the Internet became popular, underground music was supported by the mail.

レーベルへ現金書留や郵便為替を送り、数週間後に世界のどこかから作品が届く。

現在では想像しにくい方法だが、このやり取りが国際的なコミュニティを築いていた。

作品とともに送られてくるカタログ。

handwritten letter.

ニュースレター。

Flyer.

These paper media were also an important source of information about new works and artists.

Once you order a piece of work that interests you, it will lead you to other labels and artists.

Through this chain, experimental music scenes in North America, Europe, Japan, and Oceania were loosely connected.

Interestingly, anonymity was not an obstacle for this network.

What was important was not his real name, but the trust he had in continuing to produce outstanding works.

As a result, works became separated from the individual creators and were naturally shared within a single cultural sphere.

Angine de Poitrine, Carolinar, and Rubber O Cement each benefited from this international distribution network in different ways, delivering their works to many enthusiasts.

> The postal network became an important foundation that supported an era in which works were valued across national borders, even if they were anonymous.

## なぜ匿名は1980年代から2000年代に広がったのか

Artists working anonymously existed even before that.

However, the reason why its presence has grown since the 1980s is due to a combination of multiple factors.

First, the DIY culture has matured.

Second, there is an increase in small-scale labels.

Thirdly, media suitable for distribution in small quantities, such as cassettes and self-produced CDs, are becoming more widespread.

Fourth, an international underground network for evaluating works has been established.

As a result of these interactions, an environment has expanded where the ""work itself" is evaluated rather than the ""personal brand."

Anonymity is not a byproduct of the environment.

むしろ、その環境を象徴する表現の一つだった。

Caroliner created the story, Rubber O Cement let the work stand on its own, and Angine de Poitrine quietly receded the author into the background.

Although the methods of the three methods are different, they were all possible because of the same cultural conditions of the time.

## Compare the anonymity of the three parties

As we have seen, while Angine de Poitrine, Carolinar, and Rubber O Cement all place anonymity at the core of their expressions, they practice it in very different ways.

If you just look at the word "anonymous", it seems like they have something in common, but in reality, their goals and the effects they had on their works are not the same.

Carolinerでは、匿名は架空世界を成立させるための舞台装置だった。

The work is not about the individual members, but the community called "Caroliner" itself.

Audiences participate in the myth system through live shows and records.

Anonymous was a creative engine for amplifying worldviews.

At Rubber O Cement, anonymity functions as an environment that protects the autonomy of the work.

The composition is such that the sound and collages themselves are perceived as independent entities, without pushing information about the author to the forefront.

In this case, the emphasis is on ""what kind of experience the work brings" rather than the question ""who made it?"

Angine de Poitrineでは、さらに静かな匿名性が見られる。

The author actively disappears, leaving only the work in front of the viewer.

As a result, the work continues to acquire different meanings depending on the listener.

Anonymity is a structure that ensures space for viewers to freely interpret the work.

While moving away from ""author-centeredness'' from different directions, each of the three established their own unique creative methods.

## Chronology: Until anonymous expression matured

The expression "anonymous" was not created by a single artist.

It has been nurtured over time in underground culture and DIY networks.

| ------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------|
| 1960s | Experimental art and hippie culture develop in San Francisco | Formation of collaborative production and performance |
| 1970s | Punk, experimental music, and mail art with a DIY spirit expand | Values ​​that emphasize works over personal brands spread |
| 1980s | Small-scale labels and cassette culture become active | An environment is created to distribute works anonymously |
| Late 1980s | Caroliner becomes active | Develops unique expression that combines anonymity and fictional world |

| Currently | Digital distribution has become commonplace | The method of anonymity is being reevaluated in a new way |

> Anonymous expression is not a passing fad, but an important legacy of underground culture that has been passed down for more than half a century.

## What does anonymity mean in the digital age?

Nowadays, publishing your work has never been easier.

By using video distribution services, music distribution services, and SNS, even individuals can deliver their works to the world.

一方で、制作者自身の存在も以前より強く可視化されるようになった。

profile.

Daily posts.

Live streaming.

The algorithm draws attention not only to the "work" but also to the "person" itself.

In this environment, the choice to remain anonymous begins to take on a different meaning than before.

I want you to evaluate only the work.

先入観なく受け止めてほしい。

I want to separate the world of my work from reality.

Even today, there are many artists who choose to remain anonymous based on this idea.

However, its purpose is not exactly the same as in the 1980s.

当時は流通の仕組みそのものが匿名と相性の良い構造を持っていた。

In today's world, in an environment where visibility has become the norm, choosing to remain anonymous has a paradoxical meaning.

That's why the anonymity demonstrated by Carolinar, Rubber O Cement, and Angine de Poitrine still offers fresh suggestions even today.

Their works continue to quietly demonstrate that ""being known for one"s name" and ""having one"s work appreciated" are not necessarily the same thing.

> デジタル時代の匿名は過去の名残ではなく、作品中心の創作を選び続けるための現代的な選択肢でもある。

---

## Conclusion: Anonymous does not mean "no one"

匿名という言葉から、多くの人は「正体を隠す」というイメージを思い浮かべる。

However, the activities of the three groups discussed in this paper cannot be explained by this alone.

Caroliner has built a magnificent mythical world through anonymity.

Rubber O Cement has made the work independent from the author through anonymity.

Through anonymity, Angine de Poitrine created a space for viewers to expand their interpretations.

The methods are different.

The destinations are also different.

However, what they all had in common was the attitude that ""the work should speak first.''

In modern times, it has become commonplace for creators to continue transmitting their work.

That's why the idea of ​​a work continuing to exist quietly seems so refreshing.

Anonymity does not mean erasing existence.

作者という情報を一歩後ろへ退かせることで、作品そのものをより豊かに響かせるための創作方法なのである。

The activities of the three artists, who were nurtured in the different cultural regions of Quebec and San Francisco, have each shown this in different ways.

Anonymity is not a dead idea.

As long as we try to put the work at the center, this expression will continue to be passed down in different forms.
