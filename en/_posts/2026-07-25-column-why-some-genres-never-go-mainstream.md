## Why some genres don't become mainstream

### Reason 1: Requires "prerequisite knowledge" rather than "difficult"

I often hear the explanation that ""it"s not popular because it"s difficult music.''

But in reality, that alone cannot explain it.

For example, classical music has many extremely complex pieces.

In contemporary music, there are works that do not have tonality or that use extremely complex rhythms.

Still, the culture of classical music as a whole is widely entrenched in society through educational systems, concerts, orchestras, and music colleges.

In other words, the problem is not "complexity".

What is important is whether there is an entry point in society for understanding that music.

For example, if you are listening to Free Jazz for the first time,

"The performance sounds disjointed."

Sometimes I get the impression that.

In reality, however, many performers have learned deeply about jazz tradition, harmony, rhythm, and improvisation, and then intentionally expand upon that framework.

Knowing the background can greatly change the way you hear music.

The same goes for Noise Music.

It's not just about making loud noises.

He constructs his works based on feedback, overtones, space, texture, sound pressure, duration, etc.

Therefore, the more knowledge you have, the more interesting you tend to find it.

On the other hand, if you listen without knowing anything, it will be difficult to convey the intention.

This long ""distance to understanding'' is one of the factors that makes popularization difficult.

### Reason 2: It is intended to be listened to repeatedly

Many pop songs are designed so that you can memorize the chorus after listening to it once.

This is not limited to commercial music, but can also be said to be a structure suited to the structure of human memory.

On the other hand, most experimental music is not intended to be understood at once.

Listened to it several times,

Listen to it another day,

Listen in a different environment,

There are many works whose impressions change only after experiencing them live.

For example, in drone music, it is important to pay attention to subtle changes rather than big developments.

Even in Noise works, the changes in tone over time are sometimes the works themselves.

This method of appreciation is similar to reading a novel over and over again.

But in today's streaming era, many listeners move on to the next song within tens of seconds.

It cannot be said that the compatibility with this viewing behavior is good.

### Reason 3: The purpose is for experience, not entertainment.

Music has various roles.

To dance.

To sing.

To relax.

To run while driving.

To liven up the movie.

On the other hand, much experimental music emphasizes "experience."

for example,

What is sound?

What is music?

What is performance?

What is silence?

I sometimes present these questions in my works.

Therefore, the evaluation axis itself is different from that of general entertainment works.

To use a movie analogy, it"s similar to how you can"t compare an entertainment blockbuster and an experimental film using the same criteria.

It"s not a matter of which one is better; it"s just that they serve different purposes.

> Many underground genres have developed with an emphasis on ""creating new experiences" rather than ""communicating things in an easy-to-understand manner."

## "Music quality" is not the only thing that determines mainstream

### Technological innovation has changed the fate of genres

Looking back at the history of music, technological innovation almost always exists behind the widespread penetration of new genres.

The spread of the phonograph made it possible to enjoy music outside of concert halls, and radio created an environment where the same songs could be shared across regions.

Television spread not only sound, but also artists' appearances, fashion, and performances as a form of culture.

In the 1980s, MTV created a new star image centered on music videos, and from the 1990s onwards, the expansion of the CD market produced hit works on a global scale.

Furthermore, in the 21st century, download sales, streaming, and SNS appeared, and a new way of spreading began.

In other words, the spread of a music genre is not determined solely by the work itself, but is closely tied to the development of the technology that delivers it.

### "Listening environment" also changes music

Even if it is the same work, the impression you get will change greatly if you listen to it in different environments.

For example, club music is often produced with large speakers in mind.

Deep bass sounds are accompanied by physical vibrations that cannot be reproduced with headphones alone.

On the other hand, some ambient and drone works are intended to be listened to in a quiet room, concentrating.

In Noise Music, many performers consider the sound pressure and the entire space of the live venue to be part of their work.

However, many people now listen to music using smartphones or wireless earphones.

It is not always possible to concentrate on viewing while commuting or working.

These changes in the living environment have had a considerable impact on the spread of genres.

### Algorithm tends to recommend "similar music"

Streaming services display recommended titles based on your viewing history.

While this is a useful feature, it also tends to prioritize works that are close to the music you already like.

For example, for people who listen to pop music, works that are similar to pop music are likely to be suggested.

People who listen to jazz will see jazz-related information.

Therefore, experimental music that deviates significantly from existing classifications may have relatively few chances to be displayed in recommendations.

Of course, each service has a different algorithm.

However, in general, it is easy to create a structure that makes ""music that is easy to listen to'' even easier to listen to.

Some believe that serendipitous encounters with new listeners have become more difficult for the underground culture than before.

> Technology not only distributes music equally, but also shapes the environment in which it is easy to encounter music.

## Is there really a "genre that doesn"t sell"?

### A culture that thrives even in small markets

The phrase "music that doesn't sell" is often used.

However, in reality, it is often more accurate to rephrase it as ""music that does not have a large market.''

For example, if you produce a limited edition of 300 records and all of them are purchased, then your work has achieved its purpose.

The ability of an independent label to continuously deliver works to hundreds of listeners can also be considered a success.

Mass sales are not the only value of music.

Underground cultures often emphasize building relationships with a small number of followers over long periods of time.

### On a global scale, there is no shortage of supporters.

Before the Internet, local communities supported culture.

Listeners from all over the world can now connect online.

Even if there are only a few dozen listeners in one country, it is not uncommon for there to be tens of thousands of listeners worldwide.

Bandcamp, online sales through independent labels, and streaming distribution have made it possible for even small-scale genres to continue their activities across borders.

In other words, even if it does not become mainstream, the conditions for it to continue as a culture are better than before.

### "Influence" and "market size" are not the same

Looking at history, there are many examples where genres with small markets had a big influence on later music.

The recording techniques of experimental music were incorporated into film music and electronic music.

Industrial tones were applied to rock, metal, and game music.

The idea of ​​ambient has spread not only to environmental music but also to video works and installations.

In other words, cultural influence cannot be measured by the number of listeners alone.

Underground culture has functioned as a testing ground for later generations to create new music.

## "Influences from underground" that changed history

### Punk's DIY mindset

Punk in the late 1970s did not necessarily emphasize advanced performance techniques.

Rather than that,

"Make it yourself"

This attitude influenced many young people.

independent label,

self-produced records,

small scale live,

fanzine,

Handmade poster.

This DIY culture was inherited by later indie rock, hardcore, alternative, and experimental music.

Although punk itself has achieved worldwide recognition, its spirit remains the bedrock of underground culture.

### Experimental music was also a testing ground for technology.

tape editing,

feedback,

electronic circuit,

synthesizer,

sampling.

These technologies were not originally developed just for commercial music.

Many experimental musicians, researchers, and artists experimented with the possibilities of new sounds, and the results were later applied to movies, advertising, games, pop music, and more.

In other words, underground culture has served as a kind of research and development for the music industry as a whole.

### We were able to take on the challenge because we were small.

The cost of failure is high in large markets.

Therefore, works with larger budgets tend to use safer expressions.

On the other hand, with small labels and independent production, there are fewer restrictions and it is easier to try new things.

As a result, techniques that are not understood at first may become commonplace decades later.

Examples like this have been repeated many times in the history of music.

> Underground culture has not only been a place to follow trends, but also served as a laboratory for testing future music.

## Chronology: Mainstream and underground divergence

Looking back at music history, not all genres have followed the same path.

Due to a complex combination of factors such as social conditions, recording technology, distribution, and the media environment, some genres have grown into global markets, while others have developed into unique cultures.

The following is a chronological arrangement of these major trends.

| Period | Event | Influence on Mainstream | Influence on Underground Culture |
|------|---------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Early 1900s | Spread of recording technology | Music begins to reach homes | Local culture begins to be recorded |
| 1920s | Expansion of radio broadcasting | Nationwide hits created | Locally unique music preserved |
| 1940〜50年代 | LPレコードの普及 | アルバム文化が成立 | 長尺作品の制作が可能になる |
| 1950s | Spread of Rock"n"Roll | Youth culture becomes a huge market | Independent labels also increase |
| 1960s | Development of Free Jazz and experimental music | Expansion of pop market | Diversification of art music |
| 1970s | Development of Punk, Industrial, and Noise | Rock becomes a huge industry | DIY culture spreads |
| 1980s | MTV/CD era | A global star is born | Indie labels expand |
| 1990s | Internet spread begins | Information distribution becomes faster | Connection with overseas communities |
| 2000年代 | ダウンロード配信 | 市場構造が変化 | 自主制作の発表機会が増える |
| 2010s | Streaming | Global hits are increasing | Niche genres are reaching the world |
| 2020s | SNS/short video culture | Spread speed further increases | Small-scale genres will become easier to continue |

> 音楽史は「主流」と「地下」が対立する歴史ではなく、それぞれが異なる役割を果たしながら発展してきた歴史でもある。
