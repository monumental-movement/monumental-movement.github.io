## Prologue: Reading music history not as a "story" but as "structural change"

Text: mmr｜Theme: A new history of music reconstructed solely based on its influence on contemporary music

Music history is often talked about as ""the history of masterpieces.'' However, in this article, I will abandon that view.

We are not talking about "masterpieces" here, but only works that have rewritten the very rules of modern music.

This is not a work that created a genre, but a work that broke the premise of the genre.
It's not music that creates emotion, but music that changes the way music is made.

> Music history is not a genealogy of talent, but a chain of breaking rules.

## Evaluation criteria: What is the impact?

In this series, "influence" is not an intuitive evaluation. Judgment is made only on the following four axes.

* Structure: Did you change the song design itself?
* Technique: Have you changed the way you use recording, editing, and equipment?
* Expansion: Did we create a new genre?
* Spread: Did it have an impact on things other than music (club culture, politics, design)?

## 1st theme: The perfection of rock and the birth of electronic music (1967-1988)

These 10 photos may seem different, but they all have something in common.

It includes ""the moment when music transitions from performance to design.''

---

## 1. The Beatles – Sgt. Pepper"s Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967)

Before this album, music was a "reenactment of live performances."
After this work, music became "another world made in the studio."

Three points are particularly important:

* "Pseudo live structure" that eliminates gaps between songs
*Editorial philosophy that treats the studio as an instrument
* Introducing the concept album concept to pop music

A famous anecdote is that the band members were wearing military uniforms while recording in the studio.
This was not just a production, but a visualization of the idea of ​​""a work that is not a band, but a separate personality.''

> Music has become not a record of performances, but a blueprint for a virtual world.

---

## 2. Miles Davis – Bitches Brew (1970)

One of the biggest breaking points in jazz history.

Miles Davis was already a jazz giant at the time, but with this work he completely changed direction.
At the recording site, there were almost no musical scores, so a long session was reconstructed through editing.

This is important, first time here:

* Improvisation = material
* Editing = Composition

This structure is established.

Later, this idea was inherited by ambient, techno, and drum"n"bass.

>Music has become "edited time" rather than "performance"

---

## 3. Kraftwerk – Autobahn (1974)

A decisive turning point in electronic music.

In Germany at the time, rock music was seen as an imitation of American culture.
In this process, they proceed in the opposite direction of ""shaving away their humanity.''

In the studio:

* Eliminate human performance to the utmost
* Rhythm to mechanical repetition
* Intentionally removed human fluctuations

As an interesting anecdote, they later said in an interview, ""I thought of the very concept of a band as a simulation.''

This idea is later:

* Detroit Techno
* Hip hop mechanical beat
* EDM grid structure

Directly connected to.

## 4. James Brown – Sex Machine (1970)

The essence of funk is not the melody. **"Pressure within one beat"**.

James Brown is famous for repeatedly telling his band to "emphasize the first beat."

This result:

* Rhythm takes precedence over chord progression
* The bass is a driving device rather than a melody.
* Drums play a role similar to a click

Hip-hop later sampled this structure as it was.

> Music has become a physical phenomenon of the body, not chords.

---

## 5. Public Enemy – It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back (1988)

An album that turned sampling into an "editing war."

Producers The Bomb Squad intentionally:

* Layer multiple song fragments at the same time
* Use radio noise as a component
* Prioritize conflict over meaning

This was extreme for the time, and record companies were concerned that it would be too crowded to be commercially viable.

But as a result, this:

*Hip-hop information density standard
* EDM layer structure
* Pop noise music

Leads to.

>Music has become a battlefield of information density, not a message.

---

## 6. The Velvet Underground & Nico (1967)

Although it was a commercial failure, it had an extraordinary level of influence.

Features are:

* Recording without eliminating noise
* Theme of everyday boredom
* Destroying rock heroism

A famous anecdote is that although sales at the time were low, many of the buyers later formed bands.

This was not ""music to be heard" but ""music to be made."

> Music has gone from being a consumption item to a trigger for action.

---

## 7. Kraftwerk – Trans-Europe Express (1977)

A work that directly converts the rhythm of railways into music.

This album later:

* Sampled by Afrika Bambaataa
* Used in hip-hop prototype tracks
* The spiritual starting point of Detroit techno

In other words, this single piece became a "transportation network for electronic music."

> Music has come to reflect the very structure of urban mobility.

---

## 8. Nirvana – Nevermind (1991)

An album that broke the boundaries between indie and mainstream.

Characteristics are:

* Extreme contrast between stillness and movement
* Commercialization of lo-fi textures
* Industrialization of "DIY spirit"

Anecdotally, immediately after its release, it became a hot topic for pushing Michael Jackson's album off the charts.

This wasn't just a success, it was a generational shift in the music market.

> Underground suddenly becomes mainstream

---

## 9. Daft Punk – Homework (1997)

"Reintroducing humanity" in electronic music.

Features:

* Lo-fi sampling
* Mechanical yet groovy structure
* Bridging club and home listening

What is particularly important is that they chose a ""broken mechanical sound" instead of a ""perfect mechanical sound."

> "Misalignment" rather than perfection has become a style

---

## 10. Donna Summer – I Feel Love (1977)

This is the most important turning point.

This song, designed by Giorgio Moroder, has the following meaning in musical history:

* Fully looped synth bass
* Almost complete elimination of human performance
* Vocals are the only remaining area of ​​humanity

In the studio I used a Moog synth, and the bass line was fully programmed.

After listening to this song, all club music will fall within its influence.

## Summary: What Part 1 showed

What these 10 albums have in common is not "genre."

That is, the music changes in the following order:

Performance → Edit → Machine → Structure → Information

So the music is completely different at this point.

> Music is no longer an expression, but a system that can be designed.
