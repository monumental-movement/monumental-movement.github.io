## What was 808 State?

Text: mmr | Theme: How 808 State, born from an underground club in Manchester, pioneered the future of British techno and transformed dance music into an intellectual and pop expression.

In the late 1980s, acid house from the United States was rapidly spreading in the British club scene. Particularly in Manchester, the craze didn't end as just a dance music trend, but became established as a new urban culture.

At the center of this was 808 State.

They are not just a DJ group or just a techno unit. They were a group that reconstructed dance music born in Chicago and Detroit with a uniquely British feel.

808 State's music was club-oriented yet highly intelligent. He slips elements of jazz, funk, experimental music, hip-hop, and electronic music into the repetitive beats, giving the mechanical groove a unique sense of warmth.

With their arrival, the concept of ""British techno'' suddenly became a reality.

## The special city of Manchester

To understand 808 State, you first need to know Manchester in the late 1980s.

At the time, Manchester was losing its momentum as an industrial city, but a new cultural energy was bubbling underground.

Factory Records, which led the post-punk era, and the club "The Haçienda" were symbols of this.

The Haçienda was playing a lot of new dance music from New York, Chicago, and Detroit. Drug culture, street sensibilities, and art sensibilities come together to create a unique festival space.

The members of 808 State also began their activities in this atmosphere.

### Main members

* Graham Massey
*Martin Price
*Gerald Simpson
* Andrew Barker, who joined later

Graham Massey, in particular, had a deep knowledge of jazz, funk, and experimental music, and was an important figure supporting the intellectual side of 808 State.

Gerald Simpson later branched out as A Guy Called Gerald and became an important figure in British acid house history.

## Group formation and initial activities

808 State began its activities around 1987.

The group's name is of course taken from the Roland TR-808. This was not just the name of a piece of equipment, it was a symbol of dance music itself at the time.

They originally grew out of the community around the record shop Eastern Bloc Records.

Eastern Bloc was more than just a store, it was an information exchange hub where DJs, track makers, and clubbers gathered.

At the time, a full-fledged techno production environment had not yet been established in Britain. To that end, 808 State makes full use of second-hand equipment, cheap drum machines, and samplers to create their own unique sound.

### Characteristics of initial equipment

*Roland TR-808
*Roland TB-303
* Akai Sampler
* Analog synthesizer
* tape editing

Their early work was heavily influenced by Chicago house and Detroit techno.

However, at the same time, it also contained a cloudy atmosphere and psychedelic feel unique to the UK.

## The revolution called "Newbuild"

Newbuild, released in 1988, was later reevaluated as an important work in British techno history.

It wasn't a big hit at the time. However, the content was extremely advanced.

Although the album was based on acid house, it boldly introduced long compositions, ambient developments, jazz sensibilities, and experimentation.

In particular, the elements that would become the prototype for "Pacific State" were already appearing at this point.

### Features of "Newbuild"

| Element | Content |
| ------ | ----------- |
| Rhythm | Four beats from Chicago |
| Sound | Ambient sense of space |
| Sampling | Derived from jazz funk |
| Composition | Long and immersive |
| Atmosphere | British and cloudy sound image |

Dance music at the time was focused on singles, but ""Newbuild'' was an album.

This was also connected to later Warp works and the flow of IDM.

## Impact of "Pacific State"

It was "Pacific State" that pushed 808 State into a global presence.

This song, released in 1989, drastically changed the history of British club music.

Saxophone phrases, bird calls, smooth chords, and gentle beats.

Unlike the aggression of traditional acid house, ""Pacific State'' had a sense of openness and floating.

It was music reminiscent of a city sunrise rather than a late-night rave.

### Innovation of "Pacific State"

* Fusion of techno and jazz sense
* Ambient sound sampling
* Introducing a sense of relaxation
* Ambient spatiality
* UK's unique sense of humidity

The song also achieved success on the British charts.

This was the moment when music originating from club culture began to reach the general public.

## Electronic music before and after Warp

The existence of 808 State also had a great influence on the intellectual electronic route that Warp Records would later develop.

In the early 1990s, club music became more fragmented.

* Hardcore
* breakbeats
* jungle
*IDM
* Ambient Techno

808 State existed at a crossroads.

What is especially important is that it balances ""music for dancing" and ""music for listening."

This was a philosophy unique to British electronic music that later led to Aphex Twin, Autechre, Boards of Canada, and others.

## "90" and the acquisition of pop appeal

With their 1990 album 90, 808 State went further in a pop direction.

Still, the musicality was by no means simple.

While maintaining complex rhythms, irregular structures, jazz sensibilities, and sampling techniques, the sound changes to a more open one.

Songs such as "Cubik" and "Ancodia" are still considered representative songs in UK techno history.

### Important points of "90"

| Song title | Features |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| Pacific 202 | Floating feeling and ambient quality |
| Cubik | Electro rhythm |
| Ancodia | Enhanced sense of melody |
| Cobra Bora | Experimentality and club feeling |

808 State at this time achieved a very high level of balance between underground and popular music.

## Relationship with Madchester

808 State also had deep ties to "Madchester" culture.

Madchester is a general term for the music, fashion, and club culture that occurred around Manchester from the late 1980s to the early 1990s.

Famous bands such as Happy Mondays and The Stone Roses had a strong dance music culture behind them.

808 State was a key player on the electronic music side of that.

Their music was also accepted by rock listeners.

This was quite special at the time.

## Evolution of live expression

808 State also had a strong commitment to live performance.

Live electronic music performances at the time often seemed like monotonous equipment operations. However, they emphasized improvisation and a sense of performance.

Graham Massey, in particular, played multiple instruments and brought a jazz sensibility to his live performances.

### Elements emphasized during live performance

* Real-time editing
* Improvisation
* Sample deformation
* Synth operation
* Spatial production

This had a great influence on later live techno expressions.

In the mid-1990s, the electronic music scene became even bigger.

Rave culture became commercialized and club music entered the mainstream.

808 State continues to operate in the major leagues, but maintaining its early innovation has not been easy.

However, they did not lose their experimental spirit.

In songs such as ""Gorgeous" and ""Don Solaris," he approaches a broader range of musicality.

### Characteristics of this period

He has a strong interest in jazz, fusion, contemporary and experimental music and has participated in a variety of projects.

Massey continued to collaborate with numerous artists in later years.

## Why 808 State still matters

Electronic music has now become a huge industry.

However, when 808 State started, techno was still an unknown future music.

They were trying to establish that future not just as a trend, but as a culture.

That's why it never gets old even when you listen to it now.

808 State's work retains a genuine urge to create new sounds.

In this day and age, where genres have become increasingly standardized, this sounds rather refreshing.

### 808 State's legacy

* Basics of British Techno
* Development of Ambient Techno
* Intellectualization of club music
* Fusion of rock and dance
