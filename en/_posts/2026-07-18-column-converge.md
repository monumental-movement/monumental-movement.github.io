## Introduction

Text: mmr | Theme: Converge, which defined heavy music from the 1990s onwards. Interpreting its history through changes in works, ideas, live performances, and musicality


In the early 1990s, hardcore punk was at a major turning point in America.

The straight punk and New York hardcore of the 1980s have entered a period of maturity, and young bands seeking new forms of expression are appearing one after another in various places. One of the most active areas was Boston, Massachusetts.

In Boston, not only hardcore, but also thrash metal, death metal, grindcore, industrial, noise, and other diverse music naturally intersected. The younger generation, who didn't care about genre boundaries, wanted to incorporate not only speed, but also weight, complexity, and emotion into their music.

It was in this environment that Converge was formed in 1990.

They didn't simply fuse metal and hardcore.

A complex rhythmic structure.
Intense tempo change.
Guitar sound containing noise.
Vocals that expose their emotions to the utmost.

By establishing these as a single piece of music, they became an entity that changed the direction of the genre that was later referred to as "metalcore."

Converge aren't a trendsetting band.

They are a band whose trends followed them.

In this article, we look back at the history of the band, focusing on its history from the beginning of its formation to its masterpiece, ""Jane Doe.''

> Converge did not combine existing genres, but created a new method of expression itself.

## Boston Hardcore before its formation

### Scene from the late 1980s

In Boston in the 1980s, a hardcore scene was forming, represented by SSD, DYS, Negative FX, etc.

Initially, the style was close to New York hardcore, but over time more and more bands began to actively incorporate metal influences.

On the other hand, thrash metal such as Metallica and Slayer were gaining popularity throughout the United States, and grindcore by British bands such as Napalm Death and Carcass was also a great inspiration to the younger generation.

This kind of music is accepted based on the values ​​of being ""more intense and freer," rather than being judged as ""hardcore or metal."

This free atmosphere was behind the birth of Converge.

### Inheriting the DIY spirit

Boston had a stronger independent production culture than the major labels.

Live house.
small label.
Self-produced cassette.
Hand-printed flyer.

This DIY culture has been taking root since the 1980s, and Converge started its activities within this trend.

The reason why they continued to emphasize the quality of their work over commerciality until later years is deeply rooted in this culture.

## Formation of Converge

### Band that started in high school

Converge was formed in Massachusetts in 1990.

The central players were Jacob Bannon and Kurt Ballou.

Still in their teens at the time, they listened to a wide variety of music, including not only hardcore but also metal, punk, and alternative rock.

The initial members were not fixed, and the lineup changed several times immediately after its formation.

But Jacob Bannon"s expressiveness and Kurt Ballou"s songwriting ability formed the band's core from early on.

### Meaning of band name

"Converge" has the meaning of "converge" or "gather to one point."

It is known as a name that does not indicate a specific ideology or religious meaning, but rather symbolizes the musicality in which various elements intersect.

Looking back on his later works, it can be said that this name ended up being very symbolic.

hardcore.
metal.
punk.
noise.
chaotic hardcore.

Everything converges to one point: Converge.

### Initial demo

From around 1991 to 1992, Converge produced several demos and splits.

The sound sources from that time do not yet have as complex a composition as they did in later years.

but,

・Extreme tempo changes

・Metallic guitar riff

・Screaming vocals

・Unpredictable song development

Elements that would lead to later styles were already emerging.

> Although Converge was not in its final form from its inception, their originality was already evident throughout their sound.

## The prototype shown in "Halo in a Haystack"

### First full album

In 1994, Converge released their first full-length album, Halo in a Haystack.

Although it is not as well known today as later works, it is an essential work in understanding the band's direction.

The recording environment was by no means luxurious.

Even so, their energy as a live band is strongly conveyed through their performance, which prioritizes momentum.

### Musicality

In the album,

・New School Hardcore

・Thrash metal

・Grindcore

etc. are mixed.

Although not yet as complex as later versions, the characteristics of sudden breaks in rhythm and the introduction of dissonance were already present.

Jacob Bannon's vocals are also moving closer to a style that expresses his emotions directly, rather than just shouting.

### Evaluation at the time

Initially, it was not a huge commercial success.

However, their name gradually became known in the underground scene, and through their live performances they gained a loyal following.

It is now regarded as an important work representing the early days of Converge.

> Halo in a Haystack was not the final product, but it was the first time Converge's core ideas and playing style began to take shape.

## Great evolution with "Petitioning the Empty Sky"

### A work that was a turning point

Petitioning the Empty Sky, released in 1996, was a major turning point for Converge.

Compared to the previous work,

Accuracy of performance.

Music composition.

Recording quality.

Everything has improved greatly.

From this point on, Converge began to be recognized as not just a hardcore band, but as an entity with its own unique music.

### Establishment of Chaotic Hardcore

In this work,

A rhythm that suddenly stops.

complex beat.

Irregular guitar riff.

Intense dynamics.

These functions as one song.

Despite the chaos, there is a strong sense of unity in the performance, which is why it is called "calculated chaos."

### Jacob Bannon lyrics

Jacob Bannon's lyrics often deal with personal feelings, conflicts, and relationships rather than directly addressing social issues.

Although it uses many abstract expressions, the content strongly conveys anger, sadness, and a sense of loss.

This style continued in subsequent works and became an important characteristic of Converge.

### Live Rating Increase

After the album's release, Converge toured extensively throughout the United States.

They are known for their live performance, which is more powerful than the sound source, and have received high praise from many audiences and band members.

Its reputation spread mainly through word of mouth, and Converge's presence permeated the entire underground scene.

> With Petitioning the Empty Sky, Converge established their own musical style and began to grow into a representative of the underground scene.

## "When Forever Comes Crashing" – a work that elevates chaos to complete expression

### Heavier, more complex sounds

When Forever Comes Crashing, released in 1998, further boosted Converge's reputation.

Pushing the style established with Petitioning the Empty Sky even further, they arrived at a heavier, more intense, and more complex sound.

There is a sense of tension throughout the album, and there are almost no easy melodies or easy-to-understand developments.

Instead, each song maintains a tense atmosphere, drawing the listener into a constant torrent of sound.

This work later came to be talked about as one of the albums representative of the style known as "chaotic hardcore."

### Bold changes in rhythm

One of Converge's characteristics is the boldness of its rhythmic structure.

This characteristic became even more pronounced in ""When Forever Comes Crashing.''

After a series of fast parts, the tempo suddenly drops.

The once constant beat suddenly changes.

At first glance, it sounds like an improvisation, but it is actually a carefully constructed arrangement.

The overall work is supported by a strong sense of unity between the members, which cannot be achieved by performance technique alone.

### Kurt Ballou's guitar

Around this time, Kurt Ballou's guitar began to play a role in creating the space itself, rather than just being a riff maker.

Dissonance.

noise.

harmonics.

feedback.

By actively incorporating these elements, he utilizes the guitar not only as an instrument that plays pitches, but also as an instrument that creates texture.

This was a very unusual idea in the hardcore scene at the time.

### Jacob Bannon's expressiveness

Jacob Bannon's vocals are even more emotionally expressive on this work.

It's not just screaming.

distress.

anger.

Give up.

Feeling of loss.

These emotions show different expressions in each song.

Although the lyrics are abstract, many of them strongly evoke real human relationships and psychology, forming Converge's unique worldview.

> ""When Forever Comes Crashing" became an important work that showed Converge's evolution from ""a violent band" to ""a band with unique artistry.''

## Roles of members become clearer

### Jacob Bannon

Vocalist and lyricist Jacob Bannon is the spiritual center of Converge.

He is also active as a designer and has created many visuals for jackets, posters, T-shirts, etc.

Converge's works are unified as a single expression, including not only music but also artwork.

Their unique collage and typography influenced many hardcore bands.

### Kurt Ballou

Kurt Ballou is not only a guitarist, but also plays a central role in composing music.

He is also known as a recording engineer.

In later years, he would work on many works using his own studio, GodCity Studio, and from around this time he developed a strong interest in recording technology.

One of the reasons Converge's sound is so clear and overwhelming is that Ballou himself had a deep understanding of the recording process.

### Nate Newton

Nate Newton, who joined the band as bassist in 1999, plays an important role not only in the performance but also in the chorus.

The increased bass depth gives the Converge's sound even more weight.

### Ben Koller

Drummer Ben Koller also joined in 1999.

His addition is of great significance in Converge's history.

Overwhelming speed.

Complex fill.

Accurate tempo management.

And intense live performance.

With these, Converge's performance has been raised to the next level.

The representative lineup that continues to this day was completed during this period.

## Background to the birth of "Jane Doe"

### Hardcore at the end of the century

By the end of the 1990s, the term metalcore was slowly gaining traction in the United States.

Earth Crisis.

Integrity.

Hatebreed.

Shai Hulud.

These bands were gaining popularity, but each took very different directions.

Converge was the most experimental of the bunch.

They continued to explore more complex and free music without moving towards a breakdown-centric style.

### Creativity over commerciality

At that time, nu-metal was very popular in heavy music.

However, Converge continued to thoroughly refine their music without pandering to trends.

This attitude is the very essence of a DIY spirit that prioritizes the perfection of the work over commercial success.

As a result, in later years their works were recognized as timeless.

> Converge has become unique by pursuing their own expression rather than following trends.

## "Jane Doe" – a masterpiece that symbolizes Converge

### The turning point of 2001

In September 2001, ""Jane Doe'' was released.

This work is regarded as one of the albums that represents not only Converge, but all of heavy music in the 2000s.

It wasn't a huge hit when it first came out.

However, as the years went by, its reputation increased, and many media outlets and musicians cited it as a historical masterpiece.

### Meaning of title

"Jane Doe" is a pseudonym used in English-speaking countries to refer to an unidentified woman.

Throughout the album, the album is not about a single real person, but about universal emotions such as loss, pain, and loneliness.

One of the reasons why this work has been popular for so long is that it can be interpreted differently by each listener.

### Jacket design

The silhouette of a woman depicted on the album jacket is widely known as an image that symbolizes Converge.

The design is by Jacob Bannon.

The bold composition, based on black, red, and white, visually expresses the album's tension and emotional intensity.

Even today, it is often used on T-shirts and posters, and is highly regarded as one of the representative artworks of heavy music.

> ""Jane Doe'' has become a symbol of Converge as a comprehensive work that includes not only music but also artwork.

---

## Musicality of "Jane Doe"

### Coexistence of intensity and calm

The biggest feature of "Jane Doe" is that it is not composed only of intense songs.

Some songs have intense blast beats,

There are also scenes where the song progresses with just quiet arpeggios.

There are moments when only noise remains.

This extreme contrast creates a strong narrative throughout the album.

### Composition that emphasizes the flow of emotions

Although the songs stand alone, they leave a deeper impression when listened to as a whole.

tempo.

volume.

noise.

Silence.

These pieces are skillfully arranged to form a flow that resembles a full-length work.

### Maturity of playing technique

Ben Koller"s drums have overwhelming speed and precision, and Kurt Ballou"s guitar plays not only a riff but also a sonic role.

Nate Newton"s bass provides a solid foundation, and Jacob Bannon"s vocals have a strong presence throughout.

With the roles of the four characters meshing perfectly, Jane Doe became a milestone for Converge.

> ""Jane Doe'' continues to be highly praised around the world as the work that reached the highest level of musical perfection by Converge.

## Impact left behind by "Jane Doe"

### Reactions at the time of release

Jane Doe was not a huge commercial hit right after its release.

However, they are rapidly gaining support in live music venues and the independent scene.

"Hardcore like you've never heard before"

"It"s not metal or punk."

Such reviews spread by word of mouth and shocked many musicians.

The album is not a work that can be understood with just one listen.

Because of its complex structure and vast amount of information, the more you listen to it, the more you discover new things.

### Songs that show their true value live

The songs from ""Jane Doe'' also played an important role in the live performance.

Performances that sound complicated on the sound source are reproduced with surprising accuracy on stage.

Tempo change.

Odd time signature.

noise.

Silence.

This becomes one flow during the live performance, drawing the audience into an intense sense of tension.

Converge is not a studio band, but has become known as a band whose work is even more appealing when played live.

### Going beyond the framework of "metal core"

Many metalcore bands emerged in the early 2000s.

But while Converge is at the center of this trend, it has always existed in a slightly different position.

They don't incorporate melodic choruses, nor do they change direction with commercial considerations in mind.

As a result, it has come to be evaluated as a unique entity that cannot be explained by the word "metalcore" alone.

> ""Jane Doe'' raised the bar not only for an album, but for all of extreme music that followed.

## Kurt Ballou and sound production

### Exploration into recording

Kurt Ballou is not only a guitarist, but also a person who has continued to explore recording technology.

Ever since the early days of Converge, they have had a strong interest in equipment and recording methods, and have continued through trial and error to achieve the sound they desire.

In typical hardcore works, the momentum of the performance is often prioritized, but Ballou aimed to create a recording that preserves that momentum while allowing you to hear every detail.

That attitude is reflected throughout Converge's work.

### GodCity Studio

In the 2000s, Kurt Ballou began operating GodCity Studio as his own recording base.

In addition to Converge, he was involved in the production of many hardcore, metal, and noise artists.

His recording style is

・Avoid excessive processing

・Preserve the power of the performance

・Clarify the separation between instruments

It has the characteristics of

Their sound had a great influence on later extreme music.

### Integration of performance and recording

Ballou is also a performer, so he can record after understanding what the performer wants.

For this reason, in Converge, performance and recording are not separate tasks, but function as a single creative activity.

Another characteristic of Converge is that the tone itself becomes part of the song.

> Kurt Ballou is highly regarded not only as a guitarist but also as one of the leading recording engineers in modern heavy music.

## "You Fail Me"──Further maturity

### The first step to a new era

In 2004, Converge released You Fail Me.

It attracted attention because it was the sequel to ""Jane Doe,'' but it was not just a sequel.

The musicality has matured further, with an increasing emphasis on space and lingering sound rather than just violent intensity.

Overall, a heavy and gloomy atmosphere prevailed, and the range of emotional expression was further expanded.

### Heavy bass presence

In this work, the presence of the bass has increased even more.

Nate Newton's performance does not just trace the same phrase as the guitar, but also adds depth to the entire song with independent movements.

With the bass brought to the forefront, Converge's sound has become more three-dimensional.

### How to use silence

In ""You Fail Me," ""the time when nothing is playing" is also an important expression.

A moment of silence.

Aftertaste.

reverberation.

By boldly incorporating these elements, the contrast with the intense scenes was further emphasized.

Converge had evolved into a band that was not just "fast" and "heavy," but also manipulated "pause."

> ""You Fail Me" is a work that maintains the perfection achieved with ""Jane Doe," while opening up new ways of expression.

## "No Heroes" ─ Reinforcement of aggression

### A sense of speed reminiscent of the origin

""No Heroes,'' released in 2006, retained the expansive spatial expressions of his previous work, but took a turn in a more aggressive direction.

The tempo of the album as a whole is fast, with many songs rushing through in a short period of time.

Although it has a vigor reminiscent of the early works, the accuracy of the performance has improved significantly.

### Combination of simplicity and complexity

At times, No Heroes uses deceptively simple riffs.

However, behind the scenes, the rhythm and development are changing in a complex manner.

Rather than pushing the difficult aspects to the forefront, the technology had matured to incorporate them into the natural flow.

### Songs that look great live

This work is often performed live.

The fast-paced structure and powerful riffs create a sense of unity with the audience, creating many of Converge's standard live songs.

> ""No Heroes"' was a work that once again impressed Converge"s aggressiveness and playing ability, further increasing their reputation as a live band.

### 2009 Challenge

Released in 2009, Ax to Fall is one of the most ambitious releases of Converge's career.

Multiple guest musicians participated in the album, and each song had a different expression.

Even so, there is a sense of unity throughout the work, and it has not lost its Converge feel.

### Significance of collaboration

The guest participation was not just to create a buzz, but was the result of pursuing the necessary expression for each song.

As a result, the album as a whole is extremely diverse, yet still cohesive as a single work.

Converge has managed to maintain its own worldview while actively bringing in outside talent.

### Wide range of dynamics

In "Axe to Fall",

Furious blast beat.

slow riff.

noise.

quiet part.

These are placed more boldly than ever before.

Converge further blurred the boundaries between genres and expanded their musicality even further.

## "All We Love We Leave Behind" – A work that symbolizes a mature period

### A new milestone in a long career

In 2012, Converge released their eighth album, All We Love We Leave Behind.

At this point, more than 20 years had passed since the group's formation, but their creative desire had not diminished, but had deepened.

Without losing their youthful impulse, Converge enters a new period of maturity, combining years of experience in performing and composition techniques.

The album as a whole is filled with not just anger, but complex emotions that can only be expressed through life experiences.

### Theme indicated by the title

The title ""All We Love We Leave Behind" means ""We move on, leaving behind what we love."

Throughout the work, themes such as separation from people, the passage of time, loss, and change are depicted.

Jacob Bannon's lyrics are still abstract, but the emotions behind them are deeply rooted in real-life experiences.

### Perfection of performance

In this work, the roles of each member have become even clearer.

Kurt Ballou not only uses sharp guitar riffs, but also skillfully arranges noises and reverberations that dominate the space.

Ben Koller moves freely between intense blast beats and delicate grooves, giving each song a different look.

Nate Newton supports the foundation with his bass, while adding depth to the song with the chorus.

Jacob Bannon's vocals are no longer just screams, but have richer expressions that make use of intonation.

> ""All We Love We Leave Behind'' is the mature fruition of the musicality that Converge has cultivated over the years.

### First album in about 5 years

""The Dusk in Us'', released in 2017, was produced approximately five years after the previous work.

It was Converge's longest gap between albums, but it was by no means stagnant.

Each member has been engaged in production activities, live performances, and production, and their experiences are reflected in their works.

### Reconnecting with initial impulses

In this work, an intensity reminiscent of his early works appears throughout.

On the other hand, the song structure and sound design are extremely sophisticated.

In other words, rather than recreating the momentum of when they were young, it is a work that reconstructs it in a way that is only possible with the current Converge.

### Range of emotions

In "The Dusk in Us," there"s more than just anger.

Loneliness.

Hope.

anxiety.

conflict.

Quiet resignation.

Various emotions coexist in one album.

Jacob Bannon"s vocals don"t just scream, he also carefully builds up emotion by making use of the intonation and pauses in his words.

> ""The Dusk in Us'' showed that Converge is continuing to explore new expressions without relying on past successes.

## Challenge to collaborative work

### "Bloodmoon: I"

In 2021, Converge announced Bloodmoon: I.

This album was co-produced with Chelsea Wolfe, Ben Chisholm, and Stephen Brodsky.

Unlike regular Converge works, it not only incorporates heaviness, but also incorporates dark ambient and post-metal spatial expressions.

### New musical possibilities

"Bloodmoon: I" has many long songs, and the slowly developing structure stands out.

The sound that makes use of noise and reverberation is an extension of Converge's longstanding experimental attitude.

On the other hand, the contrasting vocals of Jacob Bannon and Chelsea Wolfe gave a fresh impression.

### Band flexibility

Although Converge has been active for over 30 years, their music has not become fixed.

Their ability to incorporate new expressions while maintaining the Converge-like feel is a feature that sets them apart from many long-running bands.

> ""Bloodmoon: I'' once again shows that Converge are a creative band that continues to challenge themselves.

## The personalities of the four people who support Converge

### Jacob Bannon──Expressor who integrates words and visuals

Jacob Bannon is indispensable when talking about Converge.

He is not only a vocalist, but also a lyricist, art director, and graphic designer.

Rather than directly describing society or politics, his lyrics have used abstract words to express human inner feelings and emotions such as loss, anger, and loneliness.

Therefore, there is room for each listener to have a different interpretation of a single lyric.

In addition, many of Converge"s album jackets, posters, and merchandise are designed by Bannon himself, and the group"s commitment to integrating music and visual expression has remained unchanged since their formation.

For him, Converge is not just a band, but a comprehensive creative endeavor.

### Kurt Ballou──Creator who designs the sound itself

Kurt Ballou can be considered the musical architect of Converge.

In addition to writing guitar riffs, he is deeply involved in the composition, recording, and sound makeup of the entire song.

His guitar doesn't exist to show off his technique, but to create the tension and atmosphere necessary for the song.

Furthermore, the experience he has cultivated as a recording engineer is reflected in his own performances, achieving a consistent sound image in both live and studio works.

### Nate Newton──The bass that supports the song

Nate Newton's bass is by no means flashy.

But it plays an important role in connecting Converge's complex guitars and drums.

In the chorus, he layered a voice with a different texture than Jacob Bannon's, adding depth to the sound.

Although its existence can be called an unsung hero, it is an important element that makes Converge what it is today.

### Ben Koller──Amazing drumming

Ben Koller is highly regarded as one of the leading drummers of modern hardcore.

Fast blast beat.

Complex fill.

A bold accent.

The ability to play these pieces with a precise sense of time is a major pillar supporting Converge's music.

His distinguishing feature is that he is not only fast, but also able to play with an understanding of the overall flow of the song.

> Currently, each of the four members of Converge has a clear role, and this balance has supported their activities over the years.

## Converge as a live band

### A different impact from studio works

Converge is a band that has gained acclaim through their live performances.

In the studio work, the detailed performance is impressive, but the live performance adds even more energy.

Jacob Bannon moves around the stage constantly, creating a performance that brings him extremely close to the audience.

Meanwhile, Kurt Ballou, Nate Newton, and Ben Koller maintain a complex performance while creating an overwhelming sense of unity.

### Reproducibility, not improvisation

Converge's live performance is intense, but their playing is very precise.

Even in songs with many tempo changes and odd time signatures, all members reproduce them with high precision.

As a result, even though the audience gets the impression of chaos, they realize that the performance itself is extremely precise.

This "controlled chaos" is one of Converge's greatest features.

### Activities around the world

Converge has performed live not only in the United States, but also in Europe, Asia, Australia, and other parts of the world.

By continuing to tour for many years, they expanded their support not only in a specific region but also on a global scale.

Their popularity cannot be measured by album sales alone, but is largely due to the trust built through live performances.

> Converge is not a band that has been praised solely for their work; they have continued to prove their worth through live performances.

## Conclusion

Since their formation in 1990, Converge has continued to produce music they believe in without following trends.

Starting from hardcore punk, they have absorbed a variety of elements such as metal, noise, and experimental music, creating a unique musicality that does not fit into existing genres.

Its history is not a history of adhering to one style.

It is a history of not losing the ""Converge-ness'' while exploring new methods for each work.

""Jane Doe'' is still talked about today as a representative work of extreme music, but its reputation goes beyond just a single masterpiece.

In ""You Fail Me," they deepened their spatial expressions, in ""No Heroes," they sharpened their aggression, and in ""Axe to Fall,'' they showed new possibilities for collaborative production.

Furthermore, in ""All We Love We Leave Behind," ""The Dusk in Us," and ""Bloodmoon: I,'' he shows a mature expression that can only be achieved through a long career.

Complex rhythms, dissonance, intense dynamics, and noise are not uncommon in today's heavy music.

However, even before these ideas were widely accepted, Converge continued to take on challenges and had a huge influence on subsequent bands, engineers, and designers.

In its more than 30 years of operation, Converge has continued to change.

That's why they are praised not as a famous band from the past, but as an entity that continues to push the possibilities of extreme music in the present.
